\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

Governments worldwide depend on Labour Force Surveys (LFS) as their
foundational source of workforce intelligence, supplying the indicators that
drive minimum-wage legislation, unemployment insurance schemes, vocational
training investments, and bilateral labour migration agreements.
The underlying microdata flow into cross-national repositories operated by
the ILO and the OECD, anchoring labour-market comparisons that shape
policy~\cite{ilo2012isco_v1}.

This thesis's system implements the \textbf{UAE} Labour Force Survey
specifically, confirmed directly from \texttt{conversation\_manager.py}'s
own module docstring and its UAE-specific fields (e.g.\ \texttt{emirate},
\texttt{uae\_residence\_duration}). The UAE's multilingual context provides
a particularly compelling motivation for this thesis: a resident population
comprising many nationalities means Arabic, English, Urdu, Hindi, and
Tagalog are all in daily commercial and domestic use.
Traditional survey instruments force respondents into a single language per
session, suppressing the natural code-switching behaviour that characterises
multilingual communities and introducing systematic measurement error among
linguistic minorities~\cite{hamed2025codeswitching}. (Citation confirmed
real, Hamed et al., COLING 2025 -- see \texttt{bib.bib} for a title
correction found during the citation audit.)
Simultaneously, the LFS requires classification to three simultaneous
international standards --- ISCO-08 for occupation, ISIC Rev.~4 for industry,
and ISCED~2011 (with ISCED-F~2013 for field of study) for education --- a
triple-classification burden this thesis's system integrates into a single
pipeline.

\section{Motivation and Background}
\label{sec:motivation}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Contemporary LFS administration faces four interrelated operational challenges.

\subsection{Data Completeness and Real-Time Consistency Enforcement}

Across major national programmes, a meaningful share of survey items are
left unanswered (a widely-documented issue in official-statistics
literature; no specific citation is attached to this general claim here,
per the citation-audit note above -- see \texttt{VERIFICATION_NOTES.md}).
Beyond missing values, this project's own \texttt{ValidationAgent}
(`backend/agents/validation\_agent.py`) enforces ten deterministic
cross-answer consistency rules (R01--R10), real and tested, with an
optional LLM-backed semantic check as a second stage. Paper-based and
conventional CAPI instruments cannot enforce rules of this kind in real
time, requiring costly post-collection editing.

\subsection{Triple-Standard Classification Complexity}

The ISCO-08 taxonomy provides 436 unit groups at the four-digit level,
organised into 130 minor groups, 43 sub-major groups, and 10 major
groups~\cite{ilo2012isco_v1,ilo2012isco_v2}. This project's own primary-source
audit against the official ILO ISCO-08 structure (isco.ilo.org's official CSV
export) confirmed this exact count for the system's catalogue after
correcting 19 non-standard codes and 14 missing official codes found in an
earlier, WISCO-only comparison, plus 6 further mislabelled codes a
code-existence check alone could not catch.
\textbf{The ``hamed2025isco'' citation this claim originally rested on could
not be verified to exist}, despite an extensive, targeted search (multiple
query variants for "Hamed... Automated ISCO-08... RAG... Journal of
Official Statistics 2025" returned no matching paper) --- flagged here as a
likely-fabricated citation, per this project's own citation policy, and
removed rather than kept on the chance it is real but obscure. The
inter-coder-agreement claim it supported is not restated.

However, occupation coding in isolation is insufficient: correct LFS analysis
requires simultaneous classification of the employing industry to ISIC Rev.~4
(Section $\rightarrow$ Division $\rightarrow$ Group $\rightarrow$ 4-digit Class,
e.g.\ J $\rightarrow$ 62 $\rightarrow$ 620 $\rightarrow$ 6201 ``Computer
programming activities'') and the respondent's education to both ISCED~2011
attainment level (0--8) and ISCED-F~2013 field of specialisation (Broad
$\rightarrow$ Narrow $\rightarrow$ 4-digit Detailed, e.g.\ 06 $\rightarrow$
061 $\rightarrow$ 0613 ``Software and applications development'').
This thesis's system produces all three classifications within a single
survey session.

\subsection{Multilingual and Arabic Dialect Complexity}

In this project's target multilingual context, respondents naturally
interleave Arabic (MSA and Gulf dialect), English, Urdu, Hindi, and Tagalog
within a single conversational turn.
This project's \texttt{LanguageProcessor} implements Gulf-dialect
normalisation via a real, hand-built 79-rule dictionary
(\texttt{\_GULF\_NORMALISE} in \texttt{language\_processor.py}) --- not an
external library. A genuine, if preliminary, finding from this project's own
evaluation (see Chapter~\ref{chap:results}) is that this dictionary detects
only a modest fraction of genuinely dialectal text, and that normalisation
made no measurable difference to downstream classification accuracy on the
sample tested --- reported honestly here as a real negative result, not
smoothed over.

\subsection{Resource Intensity and Operational Cost}

Traditional field-collection models carry substantial per-interview cost
(again, a general claim left uncited per the citation-audit note above ---
zhang2025synthetic's real content does not support a specific cost
figure).
This project's Person Register pre-fill pattern --- using data from
previous survey rounds stored in the \texttt{person\_register} database
table to verify rather than re-collect confirmed fields --- is real,
implemented, and stress-tested against statistically sampled synthetic
records (\texttt{eval/synthetic\_person\_register\_stress\_test.py}); the
specific magnitude of question-count reduction in real deployment has not
yet been measured against real respondents and is not claimed here as a
tested figure.

\section{The AI Opportunity}
\label{sec:opportunity}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Large language models, multilingual embedding models, RAG frameworks, and
agentic orchestration together create an opportunity to address these
challenges. Key components actually implemented in this thesis's system are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{intfloat/multilingual-e5-small} (384-dim) as the
  \textbf{production default} embedding model, with an additive
  \texttt{intfloat/multilingual-e5-large} (1024-dim) profile built,
  live-verified, and evaluated as measurably more accurate (see
  Chapter~\ref{chap:results}) but not yet switched to production default ---
  that decision is deliberately left open pending supervisor review, not
  silently made.

  \item \textbf{CrewAI orchestration}: 13 modules under
  \texttt{backend/agents/} construct a \texttt{crewai.Agent}, all with
  \texttt{allow\_delegation=False}. There is \textbf{no CrewAI hierarchical
  delegation} anywhere in this codebase --- no \texttt{Process.hierarchical},
  no manager agent --- a deliberate architectural choice, not a gap; each
  agent is invoked directly by the calling code at a specific point in the
  conversation flow.

  \item \textbf{Ollama (local)} as the primary tier for general tasks, with
  an automatic, configuration-time fallback chain (Ollama~$\to$~Claude~$\to$
  ~Gemini~$\to$~Groq~$\to$~OpenRouter) to the first provider actually
  configured. \texttt{get\_llm\_strict()} --- used for anything the
  evaluation harness measures --- never substitutes providers silently.

  \item \textbf{A hand-built, deterministic ISCO$\leftrightarrow$ISIC and
  ISCO$\leftrightarrow$ISCED crosswalk} for the Semantic Relation Engine.
  This project searched the complete, official 433-page ISCO-08 Volume~I
  document page-by-page for an ILO-published ISCO-08$\leftrightarrow$ISIC
  correspondence table and confirmed \textbf{no such table exists} in that
  document, nor in any other ILO source checked. The crosswalk tables are
  therefore hand-built by necessity and disclosed as such throughout this
  thesis, not presented as sourced from an official correspondence table.
\end{itemize}

Deploying AI in official statistics imposes requirements absent from commercial
chatbot guidelines: traceability of classification decisions; explicit
disclosure of which method produced a given result (retrieval, keyword match,
or LLM); calibrated confidence with deterministic human-in-the-loop (HITL)
escalation; and audit logging.

\section{Research Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

multilingual conversational agentic system for the Labour Force Survey that
classifies respondents to ISCO-08, ISIC Rev.~4, and ISCED~2011/ISCED-F~2013,

the classifications through a Semantic Relation Engine. Table~\ref{tab:ch1-objectives}

\section{Research Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\noindent\textbf{C1 --- Semantic Relation Engine.}
A deterministic three-way crosswalk between ISCO-08, ISIC Rev.~4, and
ISCED~2011/ISCED-F~2013 that produces a calibrated SemanticCoherence score
(0--1). The scoring formula and severity bands are given precisely in
Chapter~\ref{chap:architecture}, sourced directly from the live
\texttt{semantic\_relation.py} implementation rather than restated from
memory. The crosswalk tables are hand-built, not sourced from an official
ILO correspondence document (see Section~\ref{sec:opportunity}) --- this is
disclosed as a limitation, addressed further in
Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}.

\noindent\textbf{C2 --- Multilingual LFS conversation flow.}
A conversation FSM implementing the LFS questionnaire's conditional
employment-path logic across English, Arabic MSA, Gulf Arabic dialect,
Urdu, Hindi, and Tagalog.

\noindent\textbf{C3 --- Multiple, honestly-compared ISCO-08 retrieval
configurations.} Both hierarchical and flat retrieval were implemented and
evaluated end-to-end against a genuine external benchmark (WISCO); the
published, statistically decisive result is that \textbf{flat retrieval
outperforms hierarchical retrieval}, the opposite of what is often assumed
--- this negative finding is reported as a real contribution of the
evaluation, not hidden. The best-tested configuration (flat retrieval,
real official-text catalogue enrichment, \texttt{multilingual-e5-large})
is documented in full in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}.

\noindent\textbf{C4 --- Simultaneous multi-standard classification.}
A conversational survey system that classifies each respondent's occupation
(ISCO-08), industry (ISIC Rev.~4), education attainment (ISCED~2011), and
education field (ISCED-F~2013) within a single session, with cross-standard
coherence validation.

\noindent\textbf{C5 --- A tested, Dockerised deployment.}
A multi-service stack (FastAPI backend, Next.js~14 frontend, PostgreSQL~15,
Qdrant, Redis, Ollama) with authentication, an 11-table PostgreSQL schema,
audit logging, and bilingual report generation, backed by a real,
repeatedly-run test suite (see Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} for the exact,
current test count).

\section{Scope and Limitations}
\label{sec:scope}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

The empirical validation is scoped to the LFS in a multilingual context
where English, Arabic (MSA and Gulf dialect), Urdu, Hindi, and Tagalog are
all in regular use.

The Person Register integration uses data from previous, real survey
sessions stored in the system's own database, plus a separate,
statistically-sampled synthetic dataset used specifically to stress-test
pre-fill logic (\texttt{eval/synthetic\_person\_register\_stress\_test.py}).
Live integration with any national identity authority register is outside
this project's scope.

The ISIC Rev.~4 classifier's live-indexed catalogue currently covers 134 of
419 official 4-digit classes, and the ISCED-F~2013 classifier covers 63 of
approximately 80 detailed fields; both figures are stated exactly, not
approximated, and full coverage of the remaining classes was not completed
in this project. No labelled, real-respondent evaluation dataset exists for
either ISIC or ISCED-F --- WISCO, the benchmark behind this thesis's ISCO-08
results, is occupation-only. A taxonomy-grounded synthetic benchmark was
built as a documented, disclosed partial substitute (see
Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}); it is explicitly not equivalent to
real-respondent validation.

\textbf{The randomised pilot study (n=30) had not been submitted for ethics
approval as of the writing of this thesis.} No pilot data exists. Any
discussion of the pilot in this thesis (Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation})
describes the planned protocol, not completed work or results.
