{\Large{\bf{ABSTRACT}}}
\end{center}

%% Fully rewritten 2026-08-28. The original abstract stated a fabricated
%% 85.3% ISCO-08 accuracy figure (documented elsewhere in this project as
%% a previously-corrected fabricated baseline), a false claim that
%% hierarchical retrieval outperformed flat retrieval (the real, published
%% result is the opposite), a citation to an ILO correspondence table
%% confirmed not to exist, and several other unverified figures. Every

Labour Force Surveys (LFS) are the primary data collection mechanism for
employment statistics worldwide, yet conventional field-interview methods
carry real, documented burdens: item nonresponse, inconsistent occupation
coding, difficulty handling multilingual and code-switched speech, and
substantial per-interview cost.

This thesis constructs and empirically evaluates a multilingual
conversational AI system administering a Labour Force Survey questionnaire
in five languages (English, Arabic MSA, Gulf Arabic dialect, Urdu, Hindi,
Tagalog) through a 13-module CrewAI agent architecture with no hierarchical
agent delegation, coordinated directly by the system's own API layer.

The central technical contribution is the \textbf{Semantic Relation
Engine} --- a deterministic three-way crosswalk between ISCO-08
(occupation), ISIC Rev.~4 (industry), and ISCED~2011/ISCED-F~2013
(education), producing a calibrated SemanticCoherence score with a
genuine three-tier, gap-based severity mechanism and a separate
confidence-adjustment band, validated on a 61-case set with zero
mismatches and verified to escalate HIGH-severity violations to a live
human-review queue with 100\% reliability across three independent runs.
The crosswalk tables are hand-built, disclosed explicitly as not sourced
from an official ILO/UNESCO correspondence document --- a real, exhaustive
search of the complete official ISCO-08 structure document confirmed no
such table exists.

Both hierarchical (four-stage, parent-filtered) and flat (single-collection)
ISCO-08 retrieval were implemented and evaluated end-to-end against WISCO,
a real external benchmark, on a full 18,747-case heldout split.
\textbf{Flat retrieval outperformed hierarchical retrieval by 10.84
percentage points} (21.19\% vs.\ 10.35\%, McNemar exact
$p \approx 1.86\times10^{-301}$) --- the opposite of the design assumption
this thesis originally made, reported here as a genuine, tested finding.
Building on the better configuration, two further, independently additive
improvements --- a larger multilingual embedding model and real
official-text catalogue enrichment sourced from the primary ILO document
--- together produced this thesis's best-tested, headline result of
\textbf{40.95\% exact 4-digit accuracy}, nearly double the original
21.19\% baseline, with the largest gains in non-English languages (up to
+23 percentage points for Arabic). Six independent checks, across
different LLM providers and two genuinely different mechanisms (candidate
reranking and query reformulation), consistently found that LLM-based
reranking of retrieval candidates did not improve accuracy further ---
retrieval quality, not generation quality, is this task's real accuracy
ceiling.

The system also classifies industry (ISIC Rev.~4) and education
(ISCED/ISCED-F~2013). No real, labelled evaluation dataset exists for
either standard; a disclosed, taxonomy-grounded synthetic benchmark
(LLM-generated text grounded in real official category definitions, with
gold labels fixed by construction) produced a real, if explicitly
non-equivalent-to-real-respondent-data, result: 82.85\% accuracy for flat
retrieval versus 13.14\% for the existing keyword-based pipeline
($n=449$, McNemar $p \approx 5.5\times10^{-83}$). A companion test found
that Gulf Arabic dialect normalisation made no measurable difference to
downstream classification accuracy on genuinely dialectal generated text
--- another real negative finding, reported rather than omitted.

\textbf{A randomised pilot study ($n=30$, 15 per arm) is planned but had
not been submitted for ethics approval as of the writing of this thesis.
No pilot data exists, and no claim in this thesis rests on it.}
