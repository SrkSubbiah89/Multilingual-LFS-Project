\chapter{Related Work and Literature Review}
\label{chap:relatedwork}

This chapter develops a focused narrative review of research relevant to
the prototype: occupation measurement and coding, retrieval methods,
multilingual processing, and controlled conversational workflows.
Sources are selected for their relationship to the classification task
or survey design. Official classification manuals define the target
concepts; research papers provide methods and empirical evidence;
software documentation establishes existing platform capabilities.
Working papers and technical reports are distinguished from published
journal and conference studies. The review does not constitute an
exhaustive systematic review or a pooled comparison of published
accuracy estimates.

\section{Labour Force Surveys and Occupation Coding}
\label{sec:rw-lfs}

\subsection{Classification and Measurement}

ISCO-08 defines occupations using the similarity of work tasks and duties
and organises them through major, sub-major, minor, and unit
groups~\cite{ilo2012isco_v1}. This structure permits analysis at different
levels of detail, but makes four-digit coding sensitive to incomplete
descriptions. The skill model considers tasks, formal education, and
training or experience. Education is therefore relevant contextual
information, rather than a sufficient rule for assigning or rejecting an
individual's occupation.

Industry and education require separate measurement decisions. ISIC
Rev.~4 concerns productive activities of statistical units, whereas
ISCED~2011 and ISCED-F~2013 describe education levels and
fields~\cite{unsd2008isic,unesco2012isced,unesco2015iscedf}.
Neither a job title nor an employer's broad sector necessarily supplies
enough information for detailed industry coding. For an integrated
survey, target-specific questions and explicit treatment of missing
information are therefore as important as the classifier used.

\subsection{Coding During the Interview}

Schierholz et al.~\cite{schierholz2016interview} studied occupation coding
within a telephone interview using supervised candidate suggestions and
respondent selection. Their IAB discussion paper evaluates the approach
with the German KldB classification and discusses interview duration and
coding quality. It demonstrates that adaptive interview coding predates
LLM-based systems. Its results concern a different taxonomy and
administration mode, so they do not provide a numerical baseline for this
thesis's ISCO-08 retrieval experiment.

SOCbot, presented by Sturgis et al.~\cite{kochar2025socbot}, combines
retrieval of UK SOC20 candidates, LLM classification, and follow-up
questions when the initial response is insufficient. The published study
includes static classification and a dynamic survey implementation.
Its comparisons with a production coding pipeline assess coder agreement;
the authors distinguish this from accuracy against an independently known
true occupation. SOCbot is consequently close prior work for
retrieval-assisted conversational occupation measurement. This thesis
extends the application setting to an integrated UAE-oriented prototype
with additional classification targets, while conducting a separate
comparison of ISCO retrieval designs. It does not claim priority for
combining RAG and interview probing.

\subsection{LLM-Based Occupation Standardisation}

Li et al.~\cite{anon2025llm4jobs} introduce LLM4Jobs for unsupervised
occupation extraction and standardisation from free-text job descriptions.
The approach is evaluated on synthetic and real-world datasets at
different coding granularities. It establishes that occupation coding can
be studied as a language understanding and standardisation task without
fine-tuning a supervised classifier for every target category.
Its classification experiments do not establish the quality of a complete
LFS interview or the effect of adaptive questioning on respondents.

The IEA report by Duckworth and Fraillon~\cite{iea2024occupationcoding}
fine-tunes XLM-RoBERTa for parental occupation coding using ICILS responses
and ILO occupation titles. It compares multilingual and language-specific
models and uses hierarchical evaluation measures to recognise partially
correct classifications. Performance differs when a model is evaluated on
its source data and when it is evaluated on the ICILS response data,
illustrating the importance of the evaluation distribution. Hierarchical
evaluation here concerns partial credit for predictions, rather than the
parent-filtered retrieval algorithm investigated in this thesis. The
report is relevant to short occupation responses and multilingual coding,
although its population and collection context differ from an LFS.
Its results and those of LLM4Jobs motivate methodological comparisons;
they do not establish performance on this prototype's target population.

\section{Retrieval-Augmented Methods for Classification}
\label{sec:rw-rag}

\subsection{Retrieval and Grounding}

Lewis et al.~\cite{lewis2020rag} introduced RAG by combining retrieved
external information with a generative language model for
knowledge-intensive NLP tasks. Retrieval makes relevant information
available to a model, but the existence of retrieved context alone does
not guarantee that the final answer is correct. For occupation coding,
retrieval can instead be used to propose entries from a fixed catalogue,
with a reranker or deterministic selection step choosing a code.

This distinction matters for the present implementation. Embedding-based
catalogue retrieval can produce a classification without invoking a
generative model, while optional LLM reranking selects among candidates.
These stages require separate measurement: retrieval recall determines
whether the reference code is available for selection, and final accuracy
determines whether it is selected. An explanation attached to a code is
not itself evidence of correct classification.

Gao et al.~\cite{gao2024ragsurvey} review retrieval and generation
components and their evaluation. The modular view supports comparing
retrieval design, catalogue content, and reranking rather than treating
the whole pipeline as a single undifferentiated RAG method.

\subsection{Flat and Hierarchical Retrieval}

A taxonomy suggests a natural hierarchical search strategy: select a
major group and restrict subsequent retrieval to its descendants.
This can narrow the candidate space, but an incorrect parent decision
can exclude the correct unit group. Flat retrieval avoids that particular
restriction by searching unit groups directly. The relative performance
of the two depends on catalogue representations, candidate budgets,
query information, and the data distribution.

Du et al.~\cite{arag2026hierarchical} investigate hierarchical retrieval
interfaces for agentic question answering. Their A-RAG framework exposes
keyword search, semantic search, and chunk reading to a model at different
granularities. Hierarchy in that setting concerns retrieval tools and
document access; it differs from predicting successive occupation groups
and filtering descendants. Its question-answering findings therefore do
not establish that a parent-filtered ISCO classifier should outperform
flat unit-group retrieval.

The present study addresses this task-specific uncertainty with matched
configuration comparisons on WISCO~\cite{wisco2023}. Its conclusion about
flat and hierarchical retrieval is restricted to the evaluated
implementations and dataset. Additional ablations of catalogue
enrichment, embedding size, and reranking establish which changes have
supporting evidence and which remain configuration choices.

\subsection{Multilingual Embedding Models}

Wang et al.~\cite{wang2024multilinguale5} describe the multilingual E5
family, including small, base, and large models. Their training and
evaluation demonstrate a practical route to multilingual text
representations with different resource requirements. Such models
permit descriptions in several languages to be compared with catalogue
text, but generic embedding benchmarks are not occupation-coding
benchmarks. In this thesis, embedding size is consequently evaluated on
the classification task and considered together with memory and latency
constraints.

\section{Multilingual NLP and Code-Switching}
\label{sec:rw-multilingual}

Hamed et al.~\cite{hamed2025codeswitching} review Arabic code-switching
across language varieties and between Arabic and other languages,
including limitations in available data and modelling approaches. Their
review motivates attention to dialectal and mixed-language inputs;
it does not establish how frequently these occur in this prototype's
target respondent population or that a particular normalisation method
improves survey coding.

The prototype's Gulf Arabic dictionary is a lightweight preprocessing
choice. Replacing a recognised dialect token can make a description
closer to the catalogue wording, but it can also leave unrecognised
variants unchanged or alter useful information. The relevant empirical
question is whether normalisation changes downstream classification
under a controlled comparison. The reported ablation uses generated
dialectal text, so its outcome cannot establish performance on spontaneous
speech, noisy transcription, or the full range of Gulf varieties.

Language support also requires consistent interpretation of categorical
answers, negation, numbers, and field corrections. A translated question
can be displayed correctly while its answer is interpreted incorrectly.
Functional routing tests therefore complement classifier benchmarks,
and native-speaker review and cognitive interviewing remain necessary
for measurement equivalence. The WISCO languages, the synthetic
evaluation languages, and the five interface languages are reported as
separate coverage dimensions in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}.

\section{Conversational Workflow and Quality Control}
\label{sec:rw-multiagent}

\subsection{Existing Computer-Assisted Survey Capabilities}

Survey Solutions implements conditional question enabling, validation
expressions, and multilingual questionnaires
\cite{worldbank2016surveysolutionsvalidation,worldbank2026surveysolutionslanguages}.
These are established capabilities of computer-assisted survey systems.
An appropriate comparison for a conversational prototype must therefore
consider a well-programmed electronic questionnaire, rather than assume
that all conventional instruments lack routing or consistency checks.

The additional design problem is preserving those controls when an answer
is obtained in free text and can update multiple fields. The prototype
uses a finite-state controller for interview progression and separates
field interpretation from classification and validation. Tests of these
boundaries assess software behaviour; a field study must assess whether
the same design improves or worsens response quality in use.

\subsection{Modular and Multi-Agent Orchestration}

Guo et al.~\cite{guo2024multiagent} review LLM-based multi-agent systems
and their applications and challenges. Multiple specialised components
can organise responsibilities, but the framework label alone does not
establish a benefit over a simpler pipeline. Relevant comparisons must
hold data, models, and task definitions sufficiently constant to isolate
the effect of orchestration.

The current system uses CrewAI abstractions~\cite{crewai2024} within an
application-controlled workflow. Modules are invoked directly, with
delegation disabled, rather than through an autonomous hierarchical
manager. The thesis therefore evaluates module invocation and data flow
as engineering properties. It does not attribute classification gains to
multi-agent collaboration without a corresponding controlled ablation.

\subsection{Cross-Standard Screening and Human Review}

Because occupation, industry, and education describe related but distinct
concepts, cross-standard associations can help identify records that
deserve examination. Such associations also reflect assumptions about
typical work and education patterns. An atypical combination may represent
an error, a valid career transition, an unusual establishment, or skills
acquired outside formal education. A screening score must consequently
be distinguished from a truth label.

The proposed SRE uses explicit, hand-built associations and deterministic
severity rules. Its evaluation can establish whether a rule fires on its
test cases and whether flagged cases enter the review queue. Establishing
its value for official statistics additionally requires independent expert
annotation, estimates of false positive and false negative rates, and
analysis of reviewer workload. Authentication and audit logging support
traceability but do not by themselves demonstrate privacy compliance or
production security.

\section{Research Gaps and Position of the Thesis}
\label{sec:rw-gaps}

The reviewed work supplies precedents for automated occupation coding,
retrieval-assisted probing, multilingual representations, and survey
validation. The gaps below are scoped empirical and integration questions
for this prototype, rather than claims that these capabilities are absent
from all previous systems.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{G\arabic*.}, leftmargin=2.5em]
  \item \textbf{Task-specific retrieval evidence.} The existence of a
  classification hierarchy does not determine the best retrieval strategy.
  A controlled ISCO comparison is required to distinguish hierarchy,
  embedding, and catalogue effects (RQ1).

  \item \textbf{Evidence for integrated classification and screening.}
  A system that produces occupation, industry, and education codes must
  establish their coverage and the behaviour of review rules separately.
  Independent validation of those associations remains necessary (RQ2).

  \item \textbf{Transfer to intended languages and dialects.} Multilingual
  embeddings and interface translations leave uncertainty about answer
  interpretation, classification, and measurement equivalence in the
  intended survey population (RQ3).

  \item \textbf{From operational execution to respondent outcomes.}
  Functional checks and synthetic interviews assess system behaviour;
  they do not establish burden, satisfaction, cost, or survey-mode effects.
  The transition to a human study remains an evidence gap (RQ4).
\end{enumerate}
