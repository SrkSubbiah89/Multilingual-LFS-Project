\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter addresses the four research questions through occupation-coding
benchmarks, synthetic multi-standard experiments, rule-based validation,
and operational tests. These evidence sources measure different properties:
WISCO supports a controlled comparison of occupation-title coding; generated
texts assess taxonomy recovery; scripted interviews assess software
operation. None validates respondent experience or population estimates
from a labour force survey.

\section{ISCO-08 Retrieval Design (RQ1)}
\label{sec:res-isco}

\subsection{Benchmark and Statistical Interpretation}

The principal comparison uses the 18,747 records assigned to the frozen
WISCO v2 heldout partition, derived from an externally sourced multilingual
occupation-title dataset~\cite{wisco2023}. Exact-match accuracy counts
predicted four-digit codes equal to their reference code, divided by all
evaluated records. The official catalogue has 436 unit groups and is
distinct from the application's legacy profile. No LLM reranker was used
in the full-partition comparisons below.

Queries use the E5 \texttt{query:} prefix, unit-normalised embeddings,
and an embedding batch size of one. Flat search requests five candidates
from its profile's unit-group collection. The canonical hierarchical run
uses beam width two, description-based first-stage retrieval, and five
leaf candidates per explored final branch. Keyword anchoring supplies
the first stage on 4,100 records; the remaining 14,647 use semantic
first-stage retrieval. A strict guard rejects flat fallback and incomplete
stage evidence, with a 30-second stage budget. The encoder dimensions are
384 for e5-small and 1,024 for e5-large.

Tables report counts, percentage-point differences, and 95\% Wilson score
intervals~\cite{wilson1927}. Paired comparisons use the two-sided exact
binomial form of McNemar's test~\cite{mcnemar1947}: if $b$ records are correct only under the
first configuration and $c$ only under the second, the null distribution
is $\mathrm{Binomial}(b+c,1/2)$. Records are matched by case identifier and
reference label. Extremely small probabilities are computed without
floating-point underflow and are not reported as zero.

The intervals and tests are nominal record-level summaries. Language
variants and related occupation titles need not be independent; no
cluster-adjusted uncertainty analysis was performed. Moreover, the same
partition was reused for later comparisons after earlier outcomes had
been examined. Later configurations therefore provide retrospective
benchmark results, rather than an independent confirmatory test of a
configuration selected without access to its results. Group-aware splitting
reduces development--test overlap but cannot remove selection bias caused
by repeated inspection of test performance~\cite{cawley2010}.

\subsection{Fragment-to-Parent Retrieval in the Served Configuration}
\label{sec:res-parent-document}

The configurations above are offline comparisons. The local application
serves a different design, adopted because the enriched e5-large index
could not be loaded reliably on the available machine
(Section~\ref{sec:res-validity}). Each official catalogue entry is divided
into title, definition, and included-example fragments. Fragments are
indexed individually and retrieve their parent unit group; a parent's
score is the maximum over its own fragments, with a fixed weight of
$0.5$ on fragment evidence. All 436 unit groups remain eligible for every
query.

This is distinct from the parent-filtered hierarchical traversal of
Table~\ref{tab:ch6-wisco-canonical}, despite the shared word: no
level decision restricts the candidate set, and no code is removed by an
earlier stage. The encoder is \texttt{multilingual-e5-small} over the same
enriched official catalogue, so the comparison below holds encoder and
catalogue fixed and changes only the retrieval unit.

Every language improves over the same-encoder baseline, with the smallest
gain in Arabic. The configuration is bound to a recorded serving identity:
catalogue, fragment map, encoder revision, encoder weight digest, and
aggregation parameters are hashed in the preserved audit record
(Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}). Unlike the configurations in
Table~\ref{tab:ch6-additive}, its per-case predictions are not published
with this thesis, so the figures here are recomputed from that recorded
summary rather than from released per-case outputs. A later catalogue-alignment variant, which projects the small-encoder
query into the stored large-encoder parent space through a ridge map
fitted only on paired official catalogue vectors, reached 39.16\% on this
partition ($+0.34$ points, 63 codes). It was not adopted. A promotion gate
declared before that run required a positive gain, case-level
$p<0.05$, and no per-language regression; Tagalog top-1 accuracy fell, so
the gate failed on its third condition. The variant was also inert on the
two smaller splits (development $+1$ case, $p=1.0$; validation $+3$ cases,

unresolved. It is therefore recorded as a non-adopted research candidate
rather than a result. All figures use the same reused partition and remain offline
reference measurements, not Labour Force Survey field validation.

Because the figures above are re-derived from a preserved summary rather
than released per-case records, the live application was also exercised
directly to confirm that it serves this method and not another. The running
API's diagnostic occupation route reported the fragment-to-parent method
identifier for every query, and returned the expected unit groups for a
short spread of unambiguous titles --- software engineer to 2512, nurse to
2221, taxi driver to 8322, primary school teacher to 2341, accountant to
2411, and the Arabic rendering of ``software engineer'' to 2514 --- at
126--851\,ms per query. \textbf{This is a configuration check on six easy
titles, not an accuracy measurement}: it establishes that the deployed path
is the configuration reported here, and says nothing about accuracy beyond
the 38.83\% figure.

\subsection{LLM Reranking and Corrective Retrieval}

% Sources: eval/local_runs/e5large_{vs_e5small,with_groq_reranker}_dev500_20260824/results.csv
% eval/results/dev_selection/{validation_reranking_check,enriched_catalogue_validation_check,enriched_catalogue_with_rerank_check,corrective_retry_check_ollama,corrective_retry_check_ollama_off}/*.csv
The available paired checks show small or absent changes. On 500 development
records, e5-large retrieval and its Groq-reranked variant both classify
146 correctly (29.20\%); one code changes without changing correctness.
On 642 validation records, title-only e5-small increases from 117 to
120 correct (18.22\% to 18.69\%, $b=0$, $c=3$, $p=0.25$). Four codes
change, all for the same occupation, ``Air force captain''. Enriched
e5-small increases from 209 to 211 correct (32.55\% to 32.87\%,
$b=0$, $c=2$, $p=0.50$).

A local-model corrective-retrieval check, which reformulates the query
before another retrieval attempt, returns 31/60 correct with and without
the mechanism (51.67\%). Nine final codes change, including two gains and
two losses in correctness ($b=2$, $c=2$, $p=1.00$). These experiments
do not demonstrate a material benefit for the tested settings. They do
not establish equivalence or show that all LLM interventions are ineffective.
The samples overlap the development process, use different configurations,
and are not independent replications. Failure to reject a null hypothesis
is not proof of a zero effect~\cite{wasserstein2016}.

\subsection{Zero-Shot Coding and Query Translation}
\label{sec:results-translation}

% Zero shot: eval/results/wisco_groq_zeroshot/results.jsonl, matched to canonical flat CSV.
% Translation: eval/results/wisco_groq_zeroshot/translation_{baseline,translated}/*.csv
An exploratory zero-shot run asks \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} for a code without
a candidate list. Its saved output contains 324 records, with 61 correct
(18.83\%). Restricting the official flat e5-small baseline to the same
identifiers gives 86/324 correct (26.54\%). There are 31 zero-shot-only
and 56 retrieval-only successes, yielding nominal exact $p=0.00967$.
Retrieval performs better on this recorded subset; the quota-limited
partial run is not a random representative sample or a full comparison
of prompting strategies.

A translation experiment uses 60 development records, 15 in each of four
non-English languages. Local \texttt{qwen2.5:3b} translates the query into
English before retrieval against the English-sourced enriched catalogue.
The experiment tests alignment with the reference text; it does not assume
that translation always improves multilingual retrieval.

\textbf{The 20.0-point exploratory result did not replicate.} In aggregate
the translated arm is indistinguishable from the original
($+0.50$ percentage points, $p=0.921$), so no translation gain is claimed
for this configuration and the setting remains disabled.

The per-language columns, however, show why the aggregate is flat: Arabic
gains 11 records while Urdu loses 6 and Hindi and Tagalog are unchanged
within noise, so the languages cancel. Arabic is the only arm approaching
conventional significance ($p=0.089$ on 35 discordant pairs) and it moves
in the direction the earlier sample predicted, but it does not reach it and
is not reported as an improvement. The methodological point is that an
intervention can be selective by language and still average to zero;
reporting only a pooled figure would have concealed the entire effect.
The original arm's 31.50\% is consistent with this configuration's recorded
per-language accuracy for these four languages, so the sample is not
anomalous --- it excludes English, which is this configuration's strongest
language. Per-case outputs are retained locally but not published, since
they embed benchmark text and gold labels.

\subsection{Semantic Relation Engine}
\label{sec:res-sre}

% Sources: eval/results/legacy_thesis_ch6/sre_expanded_validation.json
% and sre_hitl_enforcement_endpoint_verification.json
The SRE was checked against 61 constructed cases: 20 expected coherent,
12 LOW, 14 MODERATE, and 15 HIGH. All returned severities agree with the
expected rule outcomes. Three endpoint runs repeat those same cases:
all 15 HIGH cases escalate, and none of the 46 other cases escalates
through this severity-based trigger. The repeated executions demonstrate
repeatability of the rule and queue integration, not three independent
validation samples.

Expected outcomes are constructed from the project's compatibility rules.
Agreement supports implementation correctness, not sensitivity or
specificity against independently adjudicated contradictions. The score
is a heuristic compatibility index, not a calibrated probability of
correctness. Hand-built occupation--industry and occupation--education
constraints require expert assessment, particularly for atypical but
legitimate employment and educational histories.

The enriched-large run trades higher accuracy for increased compute and
memory demand. Its highest sampled process RSS is 1,822.91 MiB; mean RSS
across recorded case boundaries is 1,243.31 MiB. These snapshots do not
capture allocation peaks that subside before a case finishes. A separate e5-small

The load-test artifact records 42/42 successful scripted request sequences.
Final states are collecting, clarifying, or validating rather than completed
interviews. The sequence-based success criterion does not establish a
capacity of 42 simultaneously completed interviews. These measurements
concern local development hardware; production throughput, deployment
cost, and Claude-backed latency remain unmeasured.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:res-validity}

\noindent\textbf{Construct validity.}
Exact occupation match, generated taxonomy recovery, rule-fixture agreement,
and scripted completion are different outcomes. None directly measures
labour-force-estimate quality, human coding agreement, or respondent
experience. SRE compatibility is heuristic plausibility, not factual
verification or a calibrated error probability.

\noindent\textbf{Data-capture validity.}
Answer extraction is opportunistic: a message is scanned for several
fields, not only the one just asked. This reduces the number of questions a
respondent must answer, but it also means a field can be populated from an
incidental keyword in an answer to a different question. A direct check
against the running system illustrated both halves. Replying to the
education question with an occupation sentence set the industry field from
the word ``software'' alone, and stored the sentence itself as the
education answer, which the education classifier then coded to an unrelated
level and field. The state machine correctly rejected the answer and
repeated the question, and a valid reply overwrote both the stored value
and its classification, so a completed interview is not left with the
spurious entry. The residual exposure is an interview abandoned at that
point, where the retained value and its derived code would be the rejected
text. No respondent data is affected, because no interviews with
participants have been conducted; this is a measurement-design property to
resolve before any are, and it is of the same kind as the numeric-answer
restriction already applied to wage capture.

\noindent\textbf{Internal and statistical validity.}
Repeated test-partition inspection limits confirmation of selected
configurations. Related multilingual records and taxonomy-derived texts
introduce dependence unaccounted for in nominal intervals and tests.
Exploratory comparisons lack a prespecified adjustment for multiple testing.
Small nonsignificant experiments do not establish equivalence or absence
of practically relevant effects; no study-specific power analysis or
equivalence margin was specified.

\noindent\textbf{External validity.}
WISCO is an occupation reference rather than data collected in the
conversational LFS. Synthetic generation shares definitions with retrieval,
restricts coverage, and lacks independent expert adjudication. Its results
do not establish performance on spontaneous, incomplete, ambiguous, or
atypical descriptions. Operational validation is English-only, with no
human participants or traditional-interview comparison.

\noindent\textbf{Reproducibility and deployment validity.}
Preserved CSVs, JSON summaries, and manifests support recomputation, but
metadata must be interpreted with the executed configuration. The original
enriched-large CSV names e5-small in its encoder metadata although the
profile selects e5-large; profile mappings and build/run records document
the intended encoder. The CSV and later manifest also contain different
commit identifiers, and the CSV's recorded commit does not contain the
complete enriched-large profile implementation. The executed working-tree
state is therefore incompletely recorded; profile labels alone cannot
establish an exact source reconstruction. The original artifacts are retained. Normalised Gulf
predictions and several planned expanded evaluations lack completed per-case
outputs. Local memory constraints prevented expanded evaluations and
caused instability during an attempted API switch to e5-large. The
best offline configuration therefore cannot be used as the served
system's performance estimate; the served configuration is measured
separately in Section~\ref{sec:res-parent-document}, on the same reused
partition and subject to the same reuse limitation. Its own per-case
predictions are not published, so its figures rest on a hashed summary
rather than released records.
