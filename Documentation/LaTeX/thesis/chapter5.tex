\chapter{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{chap:evaluation}

The evaluation separates occupation coding on an external reference
dataset, industry and field-of-study coding on generated text, software
consistency tests, and a proposed participant study. These evidence types
answer different questions. None establishes the prototype's accuracy or
usability in a representative Labour Force Survey population.

\section{ISCO-08 Evaluation: WISCO}
\label{sec:eval-wisco}

WISCO provides externally published occupation titles and ISCO
codes~\cite{wisco2023}. The project restructures its English, Arabic, Urdu,
Hindi, and Tagalog title variants into benchmark records. Missing
occupation--language combinations are skipped. These are reference
dictionary entries supplied by the dataset, not interviews with survey
respondents and not translations generated by this project's benchmark
builder.

The prepared data contain 4,232 source occupation records and 20,760
occupation--language records. Each variant retains its source occupation
identifier and published unit-group label. Development and heldout
partitions are constructed by this project; they are not official WISCO
train/test partitions.

\subsection{Grouping, Splits, and Leakage Checks}

The initial per-occupation split kept language variants of each source
record together, but an audit found exact duplicate titles associated
with different source identifiers across the two partitions. The second
builder uses union-find groups: source records sharing a normalized title
within a language are merged, and each group receives a deterministic
assignment based on a fixed-seed hash. This produces 2,013 development
records and 18,747 heldout records.

An additive third version partitions the development pool into 1,371
development and 642 validation records, retaining exactly the same
18,747 heldout record identifiers. The development/heldout assignment
uses seed 42; the second development/validation decision uses seed 4224.
The build summary reports no source-family or exact normalized-title
overlap across the resulting partitions. This check does not rule out
all semantic similarity, pretrained-model exposure, or dependence between
language variants. Near-duplicate candidates were reported separately
rather than automatically removed.

Development records support exploration, validation records support
configuration selection, and heldout records support confirmation.
Multiple reported experiments nevertheless reuse the same heldout
partition. Their comparisons therefore require attention to repeated
testing and possible adaptive selection; a fixed partition alone does
not establish that every experimental decision was made without reference
to earlier heldout results.

\subsection{Configurations and Paired Comparisons}

Flat retrieval searches one unit-group collection. Hierarchical retrieval
traverses major, sub-major, minor, and unit-group collections with
parent filtering and beam selection. Comparisons use matching benchmark
record identifiers and published labels. Catalogue enrichment and encoder
size are varied separately, then jointly, to examine their contributions
to retrieval performance. Results and configuration-specific reranking
settings are given in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}.

The official title-only, enriched, small-encoder, and large-encoder
profiles differ from the legacy profile selected by the live survey
route. An offline score applies to its recorded configuration, not to
every configuration that shares the classifier class.

Reranking checks use explicitly configured language models where recorded,
including cloud providers and local Ollama models. Provider selection,
whether reranking actually fired, and fallback behavior must be reported
alongside accuracy. A requested model name is not evidence that its
inference completed successfully.

\section{ISIC Rev.~4 and ISCED-F 2013: Synthetic Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval-synthetic}

WISCO does not supply the industry or field-of-study labels required by
these evaluations. No accessible dataset meeting this project's
requirements for labelled multilingual verbatim responses was obtained.
The recorded IPUMS correspondence states that the requested original
response text was unavailable to that provider. This establishes a
limitation of the project's data access, not the nonexistence of all
labelled ISIC or ISCED datasets.

Synthetic cases are generated from official ISIC Rev.~4 descriptions and
ISCED-F 2013 descriptions~\cite{unsd2008isic,unesco2015iscedf}. A
classification definition and language instruction are supplied to an LLM,
which produces a short respondent-style paraphrase. The generating class
is retained as the intended gold label.

The label is fixed by the prompt; it is not independently adjudicated
against the generated sentence. Generation can introduce ambiguity or
change the meaning, even when the output passes automatic checks.
Moreover, generation and retrieval both use taxonomy descriptions.
Performance consequently measures recovery of an intended generating
category under this construction, not accuracy on independently collected
respondent text. These cases are suitable for regression checks and
controlled comparisons, subject to that limitation.

Generation used a Groq API and was accumulated across sessions under
provider token limits. Recorded model and prompt identifiers, source settings,
and their provenance limits appear in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}.
The target combines matched catalogue classes with
six language codes, including the Gulf-Arabic variant. Actual coverage and
its distribution are reported with each result; the target size is not
presented as completed coverage.

\subsection{Quality Checks and Historical Contamination}

The separate quality checker examines script contamination, predominant
script, verbatim English-title leakage, and refusal patterns without
calling an LLM. These checks assess usability of the text and adherence to
generation instructions. They do not verify every language's fluency,
dialect authenticity, or semantic agreement with its intended label.

Two refusal responses were included in the original 449-row dataset used
for the reported comparison. They were detected after that evaluation.
The generator was subsequently updated to reject refusal patterns, and
affected data were repaired. The original measured result remains a result
on the earlier contaminated dataset; it is not reclassified as a clean
evaluation. A repaired or expanded dataset requires a fresh completed
run before a new accuracy claim can be made. The recorded rerun limitation
and the older result's scope are described in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}.

The Gulf-Arabic subset also supports a comparison with and without lexical
normalisation. It contains generated dialect-oriented text rather than a
native-speaker-validated sample of real dialectal interviews. The
normaliser's result is therefore specific to that sample and rule set.

\section{Secondary Synthetic-Corpus Comparison}
\label{sec:eval-legacy}

The earlier harness in \path{eval/legacy_thesis_ch6/evaluate.py} compares
BM25, flat dense retrieval, and hierarchical retrieval. Its
\texttt{TEST\_CASES} list contains 100 English descriptions with
developer-authored ISCO labels; \texttt{ARABIC\_TEST\_CASES} provides a
separate 30-case Arabic set. The script does not document independent
professional double-coding or adjudication, and it contains no equivalent
Hindi, Urdu, or Tagalog case sets.

This corpus is a secondary engineering evaluation. Its label provenance
and language coverage differ from WISCO. The BM25 class is also reused by
the main harness, but sharing an implementation does not make the two
datasets equivalent.

\section{Metrics and Artifact Integrity}
\label{sec:eval-metrics}

Occupation top-1 accuracy is the proportion of eligible cases whose
predicted code equals the published gold code. The analyzer also reports
agreement at major, sub-major, and minor prefixes. Industry and
field-of-study metrics distinguish their respective hierarchy levels;
coarse-level agreement is not interchangeable with detailed-code accuracy.
Empty predictions from a completed inference attempt are failures under
exact-match scoring.

Pre-reranking top-3 candidate coverage is a retrieval metric:
\[
  \mathrm{coverage@3}
  = \frac{\#\{\text{eligible cases with gold in the first three candidates}\}}
          {\#\{\text{eligible retrieval cases}\}}.
\]
It differs from accuracy of the final reranked prediction. The current
\texttt{analyze.py} implementation excludes blank
\texttt{gold\_rank\_in\_pool} values, although the harness leaves this value
blank when retrieval misses the gold code. That denominator inflates
top-3 coverage. A missing gold candidate must count as a failure when a
valid pool exists, while unavailable telemetry must be identified
separately. Affected summaries require recomputation; this defect does not
by itself invalidate separately checked top-1 figures.

The exact-match analyzer also does not enforce
\texttt{evaluation\_status}, so it can label a dry-run row as measured.
Accuracy evidence must therefore be restricted to verified inference
runs, using run records and statuses rather than trusting the analyzer's
status field alone.

For non-BM25 runs, the CSV encoder field and configuration hash currently
use a fixed small-model name even for large-encoder profiles. Encoder
identity must consequently be checked against the selected catalogue
profile and run configuration. This provenance defect requires metadata
correction, but does not alone establish that the underlying predictions
were generated by the wrong encoder.

\section{Planned Participant Pilot}
\label{sec:eval-pilot}

The proposed parallel-arm pilot has not been executed. The repository's
ethics submission log records no submitted application or completed
participant recruitment. The draft protocol proposes 30 participants,
15 assigned to AI administration and 15 to a traditional interviewer.
Randomisation details, recruitment criteria, translated consent
materials, and the data-management plan remain to be finalized through
the institutional review process.

Planned outcomes are completion time, item nonresponse, internal
contradiction rate, respondent satisfaction, and per-interview cost.
This sample is intended for feasibility assessment, not a definitively
powered effectiveness comparison. A separate 30-session synthetic,
English-only operational run was completed
(Section~\ref{sec:res-synthetic-pilot}); it supplies no participant
satisfaction or randomized-arm evidence.

Any actual pilot must use equivalent questionnaires, document withdrawals,
record the installed configuration, and define how contradictions and
costs are measured. Before deployment with participants, the known
authorization, routing, persistence, and data-governance gaps described
in Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation} must be addressed. Proposed participant
information appears in Appendix~\ref{app:consent}.

\section{Statistical Methods and Limits}
\label{sec:eval-stats}

The evaluation code computes Wilson 95\% intervals for proportions and
the exact two-sided McNemar test for paired correctness outcomes. For
$s$ successes among $n$ cases, with $\widehat{p}=s/n$ and $z=1.96$, the
implemented Wilson limits are
\[
 \frac{\widehat{p}+z^2/(2n)
       \ \pm\ z\sqrt{\widehat{p}(1-\widehat{p})/n+z^2/(4n^2)}}
      {1+z^2/n}.
\]
McNemar's test uses the discordant pairs: $b$ cases correct only under
method A and $c$ cases correct only under method B. The implementation
evaluates the two tails of a binomial distribution with $b+c$ trials and
null probability $1/2$. These functions are implemented directly in
Python rather than through SciPy's statistical API.

The reported aggregate calculations treat occupation--language records
as separate cases. Variants of a shared occupation identifier are
related, and the current calculations are not adjusted for that
clustering. The resulting intervals and $p$-values describe the
case-level analysis under its assumptions; they are not population
uncertainty estimates for real LFS respondents. A confirmatory extension
would use occupation-group-aware uncertainty estimates and a declared
plan for multiple comparisons. Statistical significance does not remove
dataset, catalogue, or workflow limitations.

For the participant pilot, the draft proposes Welch's $t$-test for
completion time, Mann--Whitney $U$ for satisfaction, and Fisher's exact
test for binary outcome comparisons, with corresponding effect sizes.
These are planned analyses, not results. The unit of analysis for
item-level outcomes and dependence of answers within an interview must be
specified before data collection; participants, not individual answers,
are the randomisation units.
