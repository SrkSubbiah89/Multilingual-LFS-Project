\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:conc-summary}

This thesis develops a multilingual conversational system for collecting
labour force questionnaire responses, coding occupation, industry,
educational attainment, and field of education, and routing uncertain or
incompatible results to human review. State-based conversation control,
retrieval and keyword classifiers, a heuristic Semantic Relation Engine,
and persistent reports and audit records support the implementation.
The evaluation distinguishes controlled coding benchmarks from synthetic
operational evidence.

For RQ1, flat ISCO-08 retrieval exceeds the tested hierarchical
configuration on 18,747 WISCO records: 21.19\% versus 10.35\% exact-match
accuracy. Catalogue enrichment, combined with the intended larger encoder,
raises the highest observed accuracy to 40.95\% (7,676 correct), although
that run's executed encoder is not established from its preserved records.
This identifies an engineering direction and shows that the tested
hierarchical restriction is not beneficial in this setting. Later
configurations were compared after results from the same partition were
inspected, so the best result is a retrospective benchmark finding
requiring independent confirmation. It is not the accuracy of the live
API. The configuration actually served locally is fragment-to-parent
retrieval, which reaches 38.83\% (7,279 correct) on the same partition and
improves on its same-encoder dense baseline by 6.28 percentage points
(Section~\ref{sec:res-parent-document}).

For RQ2, multi-standard coding and deterministic compatibility checks are
implemented. On 449 taxonomy-grounded synthetic descriptions, enriched
flat retrieval recovers 82.85\% of generating categories compared with
13.14\% for the legacy pipeline. The result concerns restricted coverage
and includes two subsequently identified refusal descriptions. Agreement
on 61 constructed SRE cases and repeatable escalation demonstrate rule
and queue correctness, not independent contradiction validation or
empirical score calibration.

For RQ3, occupation retrieval is evaluated in English, Arabic, Hindi,
Tagalog, and Urdu. Enriched-large retrieval improves on the baseline in
every language, with substantial remaining errors and language gaps.
Translation has a positive 60-record development result; Gulf normalisation
has no observed gain in the recorded synthetic experiment. Conversational
code-switching and general dialect robustness remain unestablished.

For RQ4, 30 synthetic English profiles complete scripted interviews through
the local API. This supports operational feasibility of the tested flows,
not respondent burden, satisfaction, or comparative cost-effectiveness.
The participant pilot was not conducted; no institutional ethics submission
is recorded.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:conc-contributions}

\noindent\textbf{C1 --- Comparative retrieval evidence.}
Flat and hierarchical ISCO-08 retrieval and comparisons of catalogue
richness and encoder size provide evidence for configuration choices.
The observed advantage of the tested flat configuration is reported
without claiming universal architectural superiority. Negative and
exploratory findings accompany the highest observed accuracy.

\noindent\textbf{C2 --- Heuristic cross-standard review support.}
A deterministic SRE connects occupation, industry, and educational
attainment through explicit compatibility rules and severity-triggered
review. Its heuristic score and hand-built constraints are inspectable
and tested; their substantive validity and calibration remain open.

\noindent\textbf{C3 --- Integrated multilingual prototype.}
A state-based questionnaire flow supports five languages, treats Gulf
Arabic as an Arabic variety, and incorporates conditional questions,
clarification, confirmation, and ten deterministic consistency rules.
Multi-standard outputs, authentication, audit records, bilingual reports,
and review mechanisms share a containerised service stack. Implementation
support is distinguished from independently measured language quality
and deployment readiness.

\noindent\textbf{C4 --- Evaluation and diagnostic artifacts.}
Automated tests, controlled benchmark exports, synthetic operational
experiments, and preserved prediction artifacts support inspection and
recomputation. Method labels and configuration records help distinguish
retrieval results from fallback behaviour. These artifacts identify
measurement gaps as well as observed performance; they do not themselves
establish respondent benefit.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conc-limitations}

\noindent\textbf{L1 --- No participant validation.}
Burden, satisfaction, accessibility, and comparative interview outcomes
remain unmeasured. Scripted synthetic sessions cannot resolve them.

\noindent\textbf{L2 --- Reused benchmark and dependent observations.}
Later configurations were informed by results on the same WISCO partition.
Nominal intervals and paired tests do not account for related titles or
multilingual variants, and exploratory comparisons lack multiplicity
adjustment. The selected configuration requires a fresh test set.

\noindent\textbf{L3 --- Restricted coverage and synthetic labels.}
Enriched flat indexes contain 121 officially matched ISIC classes and 61
ISCED-F fields, rather than complete coverage. Generating categories are
not expert labels for spontaneous descriptions. Two refusal rows remain
in the reported 449-record result; expanded-corpus evaluation is incomplete.

\noindent\textbf{L4 --- Heuristic cross-standard knowledge.}
An authoritative correspondence mapping was not established in this
project. Constructed expected outcomes do not estimate the SRE's error
rate on respondents. Atypical work and education may be legitimate, so
violations require review rather than automatic correction.

\noindent\textbf{L5 --- Incomplete language and intervention evaluation.}
Language results primarily measure occupation-title retrieval. Conversational
code-switching, entity extraction, dialect detection, and translation lack
comprehensive independent annotation. Small intervention checks do not
establish equivalence or a general absence of benefit.

\noindent\textbf{L6 --- Offline and deployed configurations differ.}
The API retains legacy coding pipelines whose accuracy cannot be inferred
from official-profile offline results. The Claude-backed critical tier
has not been demonstrated end-to-end in the recorded evaluation, and
an API switch to the larger encoder encountered local memory instability.

\noindent\textbf{L7 --- Limited runtime evidence.}
Recorded local measurements exclude human interaction and do not
establish production availability, throughput, or cost. Some paired
experimental outputs and expanded evaluations remain incomplete.

\noindent\textbf{L8 --- Application correctness and access control.}
The routing, answer-extraction, persistence, reviewer-authorization,
measurement, and fresh-installation defects found in the October 2026
review were repaired and covered by regression tests
(Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation}). The evidence for those repairs is
automated testing and local verification, not operation with participants,
so it constrains the behaviours exercised rather than establishing
deployment readiness.

\section{Future Research Directions}
\label{sec:conc-future}

\noindent\textbf{F1 --- Approval-dependent respondent study.}
Obtain ethics approval before recruitment, then conduct the planned pilot
with a documented comparison condition. Prespecify outcomes and analysis;
measure language accessibility, satisfaction, and time including human
interaction. Use feasibility results to design an adequately powered
subsequent study rather than assume a small pilot answers all effectiveness
questions.

\noindent\textbf{F2 --- Independent coding evaluation.}
Obtain permitted occupation, industry, and education text with independent
expert labels, adjudication, and inter-coder reliability. Separate
partitions by source occupation or respondent and freeze the selected
configuration before final testing. Use cluster-aware uncertainty where
records share a source title or multilingual reference.

\noindent\textbf{F3 --- Catalogue and reference governance.}
Expand officially matched ISIC and ISCED-F coverage, maintain source
versions and provenance, and validate exclusions and code normalisation.
Complete quality-controlled expanded synthetic evaluation while
separately pursuing independent respondent validation.

\noindent\textbf{F4 --- Expert validation and calibration of review rules.}
Assess compatibility with domain experts and independently labelled
combinations. Measure false-positive review burden and missed contradictions,
including legitimate atypical cases. Calibrate scores or candidate gaps
on separate data and select review thresholds from validated error and
workload trade-offs; raw cosine similarity is not a probability.

\noindent\textbf{F5 --- Controlled intervention studies.}
Evaluate translation, reranking, corrective retrieval, and coordination
under matched inputs and pinned settings. Prespecify practical effects
and, for equivalence questions, a margin and sample-size justification.
Report failure rates, tokens, latency, and expert assessment of changed
outputs. Measure candidate coverage over all eligible records, including
those whose reference code is absent from retrieval.

\noindent\textbf{F6 --- Deployment and configuration validation.}
Test the selected profile on hardware with sufficient memory, then assess
API integration, concurrency, failures, and recovery before changing the
default. Preserve configuration identities between offline and deployed
results. Benchmark alternative critical-tier models on their actual tasks,
rather than infer suitability from occupation-reranking outcomes.
Re-verify the repaired routing, persistence, access-control, evaluation,
and deployment behaviours in the intended deployment environment before
participant use, since the present evidence is local and automated.

\noindent\textbf{F7 --- Other survey instruments.}
Investigate generalisation of state-based collection, multi-standard
coding, and auditable review after establishing their validity, operational
constraints, and respondent effects in the original LFS context.
